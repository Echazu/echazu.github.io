🕹️ What Is time-crisis-vr?

time-crisis-vr is an experimental project that brings a fan-favourite arcade light gun game into virtual reality. It runs directly on a Meta Quest 3 headset, so there is no separate console, no light gun accessory and no tangle of cables across your living room floor. Everything happens inside the headset itself.

The project is based on a community decompilation of the original arcade hardware, which means the game logic and behaviour you remember are carried across rather than rebuilt from scratch. On top of that foundation, this port adds a modern VR layer built on OpenXR — the open standard that lets VR hardware and software talk to each other reliably.

Because it is experimental, expect rough edges. The goal is to prove out the concept: authentic arcade shooting, real pistol-in-hand aiming, and physical movement instead of button presses.

✨ What You Can Expect

  • Tracked pistol aiming. Your virtual weapon follows the controller in your hand, so pointing the muzzle where you look and squeezing the trigger is the whole interface. No cursor, no gamepad.
  • Physical ducking. Rather than pressing a crouch button to dodge incoming fire, you bend, lean and drop your body behind cover. Your legs become part of the gameplay.
  • Built on OpenXR. A standardised VR foundation means consistent head tracking, controller input and rendering behaviour on supported hardware.
  • 120 Hz target. The renderer aims for a high refresh rate on Quest 3, which translates into a crisp, responsive feel when you snap your aim across the screen.
  • Standalone operation. The game installs and runs on the headset. No gaming PC needs to be tethered to your face while you play.
  • Android-based build. Quest headsets run on Android, and this project is packaged to match, which is what makes a self-contained VR port possible.

🖥️ What You Need Before You Start

You do not need to be technical to get going, but a quick check of your setup saves time later.

  • A Windows 10 or Windows 11 computer with a modern web browser such as Chrome, Edge or Firefox.
  • A Meta Quest 3 headset charged to at least half battery and updated to a current system software version.
  • A USB-C data cable long enough to reach comfortably from your computer to your headset, if you plan to move files across by wire.
  • Roughly 2 GB of free space on your computer and a similar amount free on the headset.
  • A play area of at least two metres by two metres, clear of furniture, with the guardian boundary set up.
  • A stable internet connection for the download itself.

📁 Your First Ten Minutes

Once the download has finished, keep the files together in one folder. Moving a single piece out of the group is the most common reason a build refuses to start.

  • Check that your headset is connected and recognised before you begin. On Windows, the headset should appear as a connected device.
  • Give yourself room to move. The game asks you to duck and lean, so clear a generous circle around you and set your play boundary slightly smaller than the space you actually have.
  • Charge the headset while you set things up, then unplug so the cable does not restrict your movement once you are playing.
  • Start with a short session. Ten minutes is enough to find out whether your play space, your height and your reach all feel right.

🎮 Getting Comfortable In The Game

Aim is everything in a rail shooter. Hold the controller the way you would hold a real pistol, with a relaxed wrist, and point your whole arm rather than flicking your hand. Small, steady adjustments land more shots than fast swings across the screen.

Reloading and taking cover are physical. When the game prompts you to duck, actually duck — bending at the knees and hips rather than just lowering your head keeps your headset tracking stable and reduces the chance of drifting out of your boundary. If you feel any discomfort in the first few sessions, stop, take a break and come back later. Short repeated sessions are easier on the senses than one long one.

🛠️ Troubleshooting Tips

  • The download will not start. Disable any pop-up blocker for the page, then try a different browser. Corporate or school networks sometimes block large file transfers.
  • The headset is not recognised. Try another USB port, ideally one directly on the computer rather than through a hub, and use a cable you know carries data and not just power.
  • The picture stutters. Close other apps on your computer, move away from large reflective surfaces such as windows and mirrors, and make sure the headset is not hot or low on battery.
  • Your aim drifts. Set the headset down on a flat surface for a few seconds so tracking can re-establish, then hold the controller still and look straight ahead before raising it.
  • Movement feels wrong. Re-check that your floor level is set correctly in the headset settings; an incorrect floor height makes ducking feel unnatural.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is this the original arcade game?
It is a community-built VR port based on a decompilation of the original arcade hardware. It is not an official release and is not affiliated with the original rights holders.

Do I need a gaming PC to play?
No. The build is standalone and runs on the Meta Quest 3 itself. Your computer is used to download the files and move them across; it does not power the game while you play.

Will it work on other headsets?
The project targets Meta Quest 3 specifically. Other devices may run it, but support is not promised and results will vary.

Does it cost anything?
The download is free. The project is experimental and community-driven.

Why does the frame rate matter?
A high refresh rate keeps your aim feeling immediate. This build targets 120 Hz on Quest 3, so hand movement and on-screen response stay closely matched.
